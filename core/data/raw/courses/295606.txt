ECON 4450 - Behavioral Economics
3 Credits (3 Contact Hours)
This course integrates psychological insights into microeconomic theory to analyze deviations from classical rational choice models. Students explore how cognitive biases, heuristics, and social factors shape economic decision-making, with a focus on refining economic models and their applications to markets, strategy, and policy design. Preq: ECON 3140.
This 4000-level course has a 6000-level counterpart. Students should refer to the Graduate Catalog for the 6000-level description and requirements.
